# Assumptions Register

Every modeling or data assumption made in this project, with rationale.
Updated continuously; referenced by the methodology report (Stage 10).

| # | Stage | Assumption | Rationale / impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1 | Virginia independent cities are analyzed as separate county-equivalents, then grouped into regional markets in Stage 8. | They carry their own FIPS codes in every source; merging early would require population-weighted apportionment of traffic data. |
| A2 | 1 | Growth is measured between ACS 5-year periods 2014-2018 and 2019-2023. | Census advises against comparing overlapping 5-year windows; these do not overlap. |
| A3 | 1 | A Buc-ee's store's effective trade/cannibalization radius is ~60 miles (config: overlap_radius_miles). | Public reporting describes Buc-ee's as a destination drawing from a wide region; 60 mi ≈ 1 hr interstate drive. Sensitivity-tested in Stage 7. |
| A4 | 1 | County centroid distance to the nearest interstate is an acceptable accessibility proxy. | County-level analysis cannot capture parcel-level siting; centroid distance ranks counties consistently. Limitation documented. |
| A5 | 1 | Scenario weights in config.yaml are illustrative strategic postures, not estimates. | No public Buc-ee's site-selection weights exist; five scenarios + sensitivity analysis show how rankings respond. |

| A6 | 2 | Buc-ee's location table is scoped to official stores east of the Mississippi River plus announced sites near Virginia; the 34 west-of-Mississippi stores (TX/AZ/CO/MO) are excluded. | Nearest excluded store is >900 mi from Virginia; it can never be the nearest store in the 60-mi overlap metric. |
| A7 | 2 | Store coordinates come from Nominatim geocoding (POI → address → city fallback); city-centroid precision (± a few miles) is acceptable. | Overlap metric operates at a 60-mile radius; mile-level error is immaterial. Precision level recorded per row. |
| A8 | 2 | VDOT county assignment uses the segment's FROM_JURISDICTION attribute rather than spatial join; segment lengths use Shape__Length (EPSG:3857) with a cos(latitude) correction at county latitude. | Avoids a 124k-polyline download; Web Mercator length inflation (~26% at VA latitudes) is corrected analytically. |
| A9 | 2 | ACS/CBP acquired from official bulk summary files instead of api.census.gov, which began requiring an API key (verified 2026-08-03). | Bulk files are the same official data, keep the pipeline credential-free, and preserve raw source files. |

| A10 | 3 | VDOT jurisdiction resolution: 'Accomac County'→Accomack; maintenance areas map to successor cities (Norfolk MA→Norfolk city; Princess Anne MA→Virginia Beach city, county absorbed 1963; Nansemond MA→Suffolk city, merged 1974); 'Town of X' rows assigned to the county holding the largest share of the town polygon; 'Statewide' rows (149, e.g. Dulles Toll Road) dropped. | Keeps urban interstate segments (I-64/I-264) attributed to the right cities; town traffic counts toward its county as VA towns are county subdivisions; dropped rows are 0.12% of segments. |
| A11 | 3 | VDOT interstate 'mainline' = ROUTE_COMMON_NAME matching ^I-\d+ and not containing ramp/rest area/weigh/scale/welcome/wayside/parking/turnaround/crossover/CD tokens. | Separates through-traffic exposure from facility counts; 1,068 mainline vs 1,103 facility rows - validated by inspection. |

| A12 | 4 | ~~VDOT ADT is a bidirectional total recorded on directional segments.~~ **SUPERSEDED by A12b (2026-08-05).** | Original reasoning was not verified against the data. |
| A12b | 11 | VDOT interstate segments are directional LRS event records (Master Prime / Master Non-Prime, roughly 547 / 521 mainline rows). The two carriageways do **not** carry equal ADT, so ADT cannot be treated as a consistent two-way total. `interstate_miles` counts both carriageways, and `vmt_proxy` and `interstate_max_aadt` are exposure proxies rather than physical quantities. | Measured directly: Stafford I-95N mp 134.25-136.22 reports ADT 260,000 while I-95S over mp 134.67-136.52 reports 150,000. Equal values would indicate a duplicated two-way total; they are not equal. That segment is also at the southern terminus of the reversible 95 Express Lanes, which plausibly explains the asymmetry. All uses are min-max normalized and applied identically statewide, so rankings are unaffected in kind, but no user-facing text may describe these as countywide or two-way traffic. |
| A14 | 11 | Model attractiveness rank and actionable recommendation status are separate concepts. A high score never becomes a recommendation without passing the eligibility screen. | The model cannot see development status, land assembly, zoning or spacing. Presenting a raw rank as advice would misstate what was measured. |
| A15 | 11 | Overlap screen: a county whose centroid is within **30 miles** of an open, announced or locally approved store is treated as inside that store's core draw and is not a candidate. Counties between 30 and 40 miles are flagged "further overlap review required" rather than cleared. | Half the 60-mile trade radius in A3. Chosen for interpretability, not measured from customer data. Counties near the boundary are flagged rather than silently included or excluded. |
| A16 | 11 | Feasibility screen: a county with population density at or above **2,000 people per square mile** is treated as severely constrained for an interchange-scale site. | The two Virginia sites the company actually pursued occupy 36.18 acres in Stafford [3] and 27.68 acres in New Kent [5], so a site of roughly 28 to 36 acres near an interchange is the realistic footprint. At 2,000 people per square mile and above, land near interchanges is largely built out and assembly cost and zoning risk dominate. The density cutoff itself is analyst judgement, not a measured land-availability constraint. It removes Fairfax, Arlington and Norfolk from the candidate set despite high scores. |
| A17 | 11 | Tiering among eligible counties: priority requires a balanced rank within the top 25 **and** a top-25 placing in at least 4 of the 5 scenarios; secondary requires a balanced rank within the top 45; the rest are watchlist. | Requiring cross-scenario consistency stops a county qualifying on one weighting alone. Cutoffs are round numbers chosen for interpretability and are not derived from data. |
| A18 | 11 | Corridors are analyst-defined groupings of counties sharing an interstate and a travel market. Each corridor's membership is validated against processed VDOT mainline records, and a corridor is tiered on its best **eligible** county, not its best-scoring county. | VDOT construction districts are maintenance administration, not retail trade areas. Tiering on the best eligible county prevents a corridor leading on the strength of a county that already has a store or cannot host one. |

| A13 | 6 | Components blended from two separately min-maxed inputs (market_demand, growth, purchasing_power, highway_opportunity, commercial_activity) are re-normalized after blending so each spans a full 0-100 range. | A weighted sum of two min-maxed inputs does not span 0-100 (the county holding one input's minimum rarely holds the other's), so an uncorrected component exerts less influence than its nominal weight implies - measured: `growth` 7.9% actual vs 10.0% nominal under balanced weights. Rescaling is monotonic (no reordering within a component) and enforced by the `scores_span_full_range` check. Correcting it moved no county in or out of the balanced top 15 (max shift 2 positions). |
